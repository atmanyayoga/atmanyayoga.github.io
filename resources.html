Foundations

What is Traditional Hatha Yoga?

Traditional Hatha Yoga is a disciplined path that works with the body, breath and mind. Its classical progression is described through eight limbs, cultivated together over time:

  • yama — ethical restraints and principles for living with awareness.
  • niyama — personal observances that build discipline and inner steadiness.
  • āsana — physical postures that develop strength, stability and flexibility.
  • prāṇāyāma — breath regulation that calms the mind and deepens awareness.
  • pratyāhāra — drawing the senses inward to support calm, focused attention.
  • dhāraṇā — concentration that nurtures clarity and a steady mind.
  • dhyāna — meditation, a sustained and effortless awareness.
  • samādhi — absorption, a state of deep stillness and integration.

Practised together, these limbs cultivate steadiness, awareness and inner balance.

Breath

Breathing basics for beginners

Begin with slow, gentle nasal breathing. Let the inhale and exhale be smooth and unforced. In practice, breath awareness supports calm focus and helps you move with greater ease—always staying within a comfortable range.

Consistency

Building a steady home practice

Consistency matters more than intensity. Choose a quiet space, practise at a regular time, keep sessions simple, and listen to your body. Even a few mindful minutes daily can nurture clarity, discipline and wellbeing.

Preparation

Setting up for online classes

Use a stable internet connection, a yoga mat, and a quiet, well-lit space where you can move safely. Keep water nearby, wear comfortable clothing, and join a few minutes early to settle before the session begins.

Mindset

Progress is gradual and personal

Yoga is not about perfect shapes or comparison. Each body and each day is different. Approach practice with patience, observe sensations with care, and allow strength, flexibility and focus to develop steadily.

Wellbeing

Practising with care

Move within your capacity, avoid forcing postures, and inform the teacher of injuries or medical concerns. Yoga can support overall wellbeing, but it is not a substitute for medical advice or treatment.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Helpful answers for new and continuing students.

Is this suitable for beginners?

Yes. Ātmanya Yoga offers beginner batches with small group guidance. Sessions are structured to help you learn steadily and practise safely within your capacity.

What happens in a typical 90-minute class?

A session typically includes centering and breath awareness, yama (ethical restraints), niyama (observances), āsana (postures), prāṇāyāma (breathing practices), dhāraṇā (concentration) dhyāna (meditation) and samādhi (absorption), with time for guidance and integration.

Can I try a class before joining?

Yes. Students may attend one free trial class before enrolling in a membership. You can request a trial through the Contact & Registration form.

Which batch / time zone should I choose?

Choose the batch that best matches your time zone and availability: India batches follow IST and Europe batches follow CET/CEST. Batch timings are listed as “To be confirmed” until published; please enquire for current availability.

What if I have an injury or medical concern?

Please inform the teacher of any relevant injuries, medical limitations or physical concerns before participating. Practise gently, avoid forcing postures, and seek medical advice where appropriate.

How do online classes work?

Classes are conducted live online in small groups (via Google Meet). After registration and payment confirmation, you will receive enrolment confirmation and class details by email.

What are the fees and payment options?

Fee options are listed for the India and Europe time zones (IST and CET/CEST), with monthly, quarterly, half-yearly and annual options. Payment can be made through UPI and details are shared by email after registration. See the Membership page for full details.
